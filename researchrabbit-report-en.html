Overview

ResearchRabbit is an online tool that helps researchers and students discover research faster, stay organized, and build a deeper understanding of their field. It turns the literature-review process into an iterative, visual, "follow-your-curiosity" experience rather than a flood of flat search-result lists.

How it works

ResearchRabbit's core engine is citation-network-based discovery. The flow is:

  1. Start with a seed — choose a paper, a set of keywords, a topic, or an author. (You can also import papers from an existing library, e.g. Zotero.)
  2. Explore connections — ResearchRabbit searches the local network of citations around your seeds (all citations and references of the initial articles) and brings back the highest-quality, most-connected research.
  3. Iterate — add the papers you find most relevant; ResearchRabbit learns from your choices and surfaces even better results, uncovering new directions as you go.
  4. Organize — save articles into collections with labels and colors; everything stays structured as the project grows.
Recommendations are calculated from the articles that are the most influential in the citation network, where influence comes from both the number and quality of citations. This is why a result is highly related to your topic: it connects not just to one paper you know, but is deeply interconnected to the whole local network.

Search perspectives / modes

  • Similar Work — candidates chosen from all types of connection to your seeds.
  • Earlier Work — explore the references of specific papers (what they built on).
  • Later Work — explore research published after your seeds (what built on them).
  • Citations & references — view and visualize the references and citations of any paper.
  • Explore authors — find key contributors and their work (These Authors / Other Related Authors).

Seed search (when you start from a keyword, a DOI, or a paper title) retrieves relevant papers from the 310M+ database using industry-standard search infrastructure with precomputed lookup indexes and graph-based methods.

Quality & maintenance

  • Updated weekly for accuracy.
  • Prioritizes the most recent, highest-cited versions of articles.
  • Only indexes articles with Open Access metadata (title, authors, date, abstract, references & citations made publicly accessible by the publisher) — this is a legal requirement. Note: "Open Access metadata" is different from the article itself being Open Access; the DB contains both OA and closed-access articles.
  • Low/incomplete-metadata articles are now shown (with a caution) so you can save and manually update them, even though they can't be used by the search algorithm.
Why a paper might be missing: no Open Access metadata; not indexed by Semantic Scholar / OpenAlex / Crossref; or too new and not yet updated. Why citations/references can look lower: different counting vs. Google Scholar, missing OA metadata, indexing delays for very new/very old-to-recent links, or a different version/DOI.

Search algorithm

Every ResearchRabbit search starts from seed articles, which drive two things: which articles are candidates and how candidates are ranked.

1) Choosing candidates

Candidates are articles "connected" to your seeds. A connection can mean:

  • One cites the other (most common).
  • They share a reference or citation (co-citation).
  • Shared authors / co-authorships.
  • Semantic similarity inferred from titles and/or abstracts.

From the seeds ResearchRabbit compiles a candidate list that can grow to hundreds of thousands of articles — far too many to browse, hence the next step.

2) Ranking candidates

A scoring algorithm weighs each candidate's relevance to the seed set. Relevance = connectedness (a mix of shared citations/references, authorships, and semantic similarity), with additional math to prevent unfair promotion/demotion. The same logic applies when searching for authors.

Basic search settings

  • Articles: Similar (all connection types) · References only · Citations only.
  • Authors: These Authors (from seed authors) · Other Related Authors (from related articles' author lists).

Advanced Search filters (RR+)

  • Keywords — loose phrase match in title/abstract; now supports boolean operators AND, OR, NOT and quoted exact matches. Keyword search uses the Google Scholar search engine.
  • Publication Date — within a date range.
  • SJR Quartiles — journal must be in selected quartiles.
  • Journal H-Index — journal (not author) H-Index range.
  • Open Access — must have an OA PDF.
  • Retractions — include/exclude retracted articles.
The result-ranking process is currently fixed and not fine-tunable (lots of "magic numbers"); the team is open to adding controls in future.

How ResearchRabbit uses AI

  • Uses network-based algorithms to support search and discovery — recommendations are based on citation and authorship relationships within the scholarly literature.
  • ResearchRabbit does NOT use large language models to power its core recommendation algorithms. It relies on first- and second-degree connections across the research network, grounded in the structure of the literature rather than content generation.
  • Prioritizes transparent, explainable methods, and is designed to be efficient (avoids unnecessary compute-heavy features).
  • Responsible-AI considerations: privacy & data protection, explainability, accuracy & reliability, environmental impact, and whether a feature genuinely improves the research experience. They aim to adopt technology deliberately — "usefulness over hype."
